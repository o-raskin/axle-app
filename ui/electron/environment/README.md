# Offline night-street materials

The city geometry and material integration are original Axle code. The photographed material maps in `textures/` and HDR panorama in `lighting/` are third-party **CC0 1.0 Universal** assets from [Poly Haven](https://polyhaven.com/). They are public-domain dedicated, permit commercial use and redistribution, and impose no attribution or share-alike requirement. The original authors are credited below voluntarily.

| Material | Source and author | Maps | Physical tile |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Weathered brick | [Brick Wall 10](https://polyhaven.com/a/brick_wall_10), Dimitrios Savva | 2048 × 2048 albedo, OpenGL tangent normal, roughness | 1.9 × 1.9 m |
| Gritty road asphalt | [Asphalt 02](https://polyhaven.com/a/asphalt_02), Rob Tuytel | 2048 × 2048 albedo, OpenGL tangent normal, roughness | 3 × 3 m |

The 12 JPEG surface files total 20,772,963 bytes. The additional [Modern Buildings Night](https://polyhaven.com/a/modern_buildings_night) panorama by Greg Zaal is an original 1024 × 512 Radiance RGBE HDR download (1,655,510 bytes). It supplies night-city radiance for environment lighting and reflections only; it is not displayed as the scene background. The original HDR file is retained, and the renderer uses half-float HDR with exposure calibrated to its night rig. These are the original individual downloads, not recompressed screenshots or preview renders. `provenance.json` records source URLs, source MD5, local SHA-256, author, dimensions and physical size. The license declaration was verified at each asset page and at [Poly Haven's asset license](https://polyhaven.com/license) on 2026-10-02. `CC0-1.0.txt` contains the legal code from [Creative Commons](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/legalcode.txt).

The renderer bundles the maps locally with fingerprinted names; installed builds make no network requests to Poly Haven. Materials begin with an original procedural fallback and upgrade all three channels together after local decoding. Their texture references and physical transforms remain stable. Failed decoding keeps the fallback, and late completion never revives a disposed scene. Both fallback and photo maps are owned per scene, while decoded CPU pixel data is shared across contexts.

Use `StreetSurface.tileSize` to keep the photographed brick, aggregate and scratches at their authored physical scale. Albedo is sRGB; normal and roughness are linear data. Anisotropic filtering and mipmaps preserve details at grazing angles. The road's wetness is a separate material response, not baked lighting in the downloaded albedo. Scene lighting, reflection and color grading are original code inspired by a cool steel-blue night palette with restrained warm sodium accents; no film frames, box art, logos or branded city textures are included.

Approximate memory at full resolution: the four decoded RGB map sets use 120 MiB as RGBA CPU pixels, and 160 MiB of GPU texture storage with mipmaps. The painted road-marking fallback adds 3 MiB CPU / 4 MiB GPU. Each material family shares its maps within the street scene. The night panorama adds 4 MiB when decoded as half-float RGBA, plus the renderer’s filtered environment map. Actual device performance still requires hardware testing.

The original volumetric and shadow shaders introduce no additional downloaded assets or NVIDIA libraries. They use ray-marched single scattering and percentage-closer soft shadows (PCSS); the algorithm is described in the [original PCSS paper](https://developer.download.nvidia.com/shaderlibrary/docs/shadow_PCSS.pdf). They target the existing cross-platform WebGL2 renderer rather than a hardware ray-tracing API. Volumetric fog reuses actual beauty depth and one cached key shadow map, with a 640px maximum target and 24 integration intervals. Headlight visible-depth occlusion is a finite-thickness approximation; it cannot trace hidden or offscreen geometry. See the Electron README for rendering budgets and fallback behavior.
